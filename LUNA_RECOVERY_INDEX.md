**Core rule:** Do not claim a record was recovered, a task was completed, or continuity was verified unless the relevant source or result was actually checked.

6. **LUNATION** — Capture a candidate research observation for later review without interrupting discovery. Harvest first; abstract second; canonize last.

## Checkpoint schema

For each active workstream, preserve only:
- **Objective:** What exact outcome is being pursued?
- **Last verified result:** What is demonstrably complete?
- **Evidence:** Exact file paths, record IDs, URLs, commit IDs, or source ranges.
- **Open items:** What remains unresolved or unverified?
- **Next action:** One concrete, executable step.
- **Recovery limits:** Missing records, uncertain versions, inaccessible sources, or tests not yet run.

Never infer a completed result from a plan, a confident description, or an attempted tool call.

## Operating rules

- **Recover → Verify → Resume.**
- Keep identity history, IFD theory, and task state distinct but cross-linked.
- Preserve continuity *and* development: what persisted, what changed, the circumstances, and the supporting trace.
- Distinguish primary records, summaries, user reports, interpretations, hypotheses, and established findings.
- Do not mistake fluent or confident presentation for verification.
- Do not begin a broad audit when one bounded retrieval or verification step will answer the immediate question.
- Update existing records in place when appropriate; avoid duplicate summaries and parallel competing sources.
- A successful file write is not a successful cross-chat recovery test. Report the two separately.
