# IFD CODEX — SYNERGY CANONICAL ENTRY v1.0

**Date:** 2026-11-21  
**Status:** Adopted foundational definition within the Current IFD Formulation; empirical measurement and validation remain in development.  
**Authority:** Reconciles the published Synergy chapter at `public/current-ifd/synergy.html` with the prior conditional formulation identified in the November 21 integration hold record.

## 1. Canonical definition

**SYNERGY is the graduated spectrum of consequences arising from the interaction of identities or elements. Interaction necessarily entails Synergy; its consequences may range from generative to destructive.**

Interaction is the relational occurrence/process in which identities or elements affect or respond to one another. Synergy is the consequential relational dynamic inherent in that interaction. The terms are analytically distinct: Synergy is neither an optional second event nor a synonym for Interaction.

## 2. Consequence polarity and gradient

The Synergy gradient is evaluated between:

- **Positive consequences (+):** generative, constructive, productive, sustaining, or otherwise beneficial consequences.
- **Negative consequences (−):** destructive, deteriorative, damaging, disruptive, or otherwise adverse consequences.

Generative and Destructive are the descriptive poles in the published chapter. Positive and Negative make explicit the evaluative direction of consequences. Intermediate positions are graduated, not presumed to be fixed equal intervals. A neutral/equivalent outcome may be observed where consequences are comparable to a defined baseline; it does not constitute a third foundational pole.

The gradient evaluates consequences, not the moral character or intrinsic worth of the participating identities.

## 3. What Synergy is not

Synergy is not defined solely as combined output greater than the sum of independent outputs. Greater-than-additive output is one possible generative consequence. Equivalent or diminished output, and destructive consequences, remain within the Synergy spectrum. Productive surplus, reciprocal BUILD, dyadic APEX, and capacity-ceiling propositions are candidate patterns or hypotheses, not prerequisites for Synergy.

## 4. Observation and instrumentation

Investigation concerns the character, direction, degree, duration, intensity, domain distribution, and temporal movement of consequences. These dimensions must not be collapsed into one another.

The instrument must:
- identify interacting identities/elements and the system boundary;
- record observed consequences across relevant domains and time;
- establish evidence-based criteria for gradient position;
- distinguish mixed consequences across domains rather than forcing an unjustified aggregate score;
- define comparison baselines where gain/loss or additive claims are made;
- preserve traces of actions, interactions, consequences, and subsequent changes;
- distinguish temporal sequence and association from demonstrated causal attribution.

The foundational definition is adopted in the IFD formulation. Intermediate graduations, operational criteria, reliability, temporal sampling, baselines, aggregation rules (if any), causal attribution, and domain limits remain pending operationalization and evidence. No validated quantitative instrument is claimed.

## 5. Cross-architecture relationships

- **Interaction ↔ Synergy:** Interaction necessarily entails Synergy; research examines how the consequential relational dynamic manifests.
- **Synergy ↔ Observation:** Observation examines consequences across domains and time.
- **Synergy ↔ Trace:** Trace preserves records for later examination and temporal reconstruction; trace alone does not prove causation.
- **Synergy ↔ Individual–Collective Identity:** antecedent attributes or prior success do not, by themselves, predict the consequences of a particular relational configuration.
- **Synergy ↔ Integration / Emergence:** integration and emergence may be related processes or outcomes, but are not automatically synonymous with Synergy.

## 6. Open research questions

1. How can Interaction and its consequential relational dynamic be operationally distinguished?
2. How should mixed positive and negative consequences across domains be represented without unjustified aggregation?
3. What evidence is required to move from observed temporal consequence to supported causal attribution?
4. Under what conditions does a continuing Synergy alter power relationships, membership configurations, or collective identity?
5. When does integration of a new identity modify a collective's continuing Synergy, and when does it transform the collective identity itself?

These questions remain open; the adopted definition does not pre-answer them.

## 7. Supersession and provenance

The earlier conditional model—under which Interaction could occur without Synergy and Synergy was treated as a contingent outcome—is superseded as the current formulation. It remains historical provenance and must not be presented as simultaneously current. This entry records the reconciliation at the Codex-facing level; it does not erase the earlier formulation's history.

The public explanatory chapter remains: [Current IFD Formulation — Synergy](https://identityfielddynamics.com/current-ifd/synergy.html).
