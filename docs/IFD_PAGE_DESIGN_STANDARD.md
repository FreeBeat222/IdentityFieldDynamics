# IFD Page Design Standard

**Status:** Standing project-wide design standard  
**Applies to:** New and revised public-facing Identity Field Dynamics (IFD) pages  
**Purpose:** Make IFD content clear, coherent, and genuinely readable.

## Governing principle

**The content carries the authority; the typography serves the content.**

IFD pages are research and discipline documents, not promotional billboards. Their visual design should help readers enter, follow, and understand the material. Typography should support the reading experience rather than compete with it.

## Typography

- **Preferred type family:** Aptos.
- Use appropriate Aptos family variants and weights—such as Aptos, Aptos Display, and semibold/bold weights—to establish emphasis and hierarchy while keeping the page typographically coherent.
- Aptos availability can vary by device. Use a sensible fallback stack, such as Segoe UI and sans-serif.
- Choose font sizes according to the needs of the content. The previously discussed 14 px, 16 px, and 24 px sizes are examples, **not a fixed limit or mandatory scale**.
- Use as many sizes as the page reasonably needs, while maintaining proportion, consistency, and a clear hierarchy.
- Body text must remain comfortable to read. Headings should orient the reader without overwhelming the report.
- Avoid oversized titles, crushing headlines, abrupt scale jumps, and excessive typographic emphasis.

## Visual hierarchy and composition

- Make the reading order immediately apparent.
- Keep headings, body text, captions, navigation, diagrams, and supporting details in proportion to their roles.
- Maintain consistent line lengths, spacing, alignment, and emphasis across related sections.
- Use color deliberately and consistently. Avoid competing or dissonant text colors; reserve accent colors for meaningful emphasis.
- Keep decoration, banners, cards, and interface elements subordinate to the content.
- Prefer a restrained, coherent presentation over landing-page theatrics.

## Practical test

Before considering a page's design complete, ask:

1. Can a reader comfortably read the content without fighting the layout?
2. Is the hierarchy clear without any headline overpowering the material?
3. Do typography, color, spacing, and alignment feel like one coherent system?
4. Does every visual element help the reader, or is it merely calling attention to itself?

This is a usability and composition check, not a demand for exhaustive QA.

## Governing direction

**No arbitrary font-size ceiling. No crushing headlines. No typographic circus.**

Design for reading. Preserve the authority of the content. Let the typography do its job and then get out of the reader's way.
