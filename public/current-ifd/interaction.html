01 / The dimension

What INTERACTION means in IFD

INTERACTION concerns identity in relationship. It examines encounters and engagements among distinct identities, their Actions, other entities, and relevant conditions. It is the relational dimension of AIIST: the point at which an identity is not considered only in isolation but in relation to what it encounters and how the encounter unfolds.

Interaction is not restricted to a two-person relationship. It may occur between individuals, among multiple identities, between an identity and an event or environment, or within larger systems of entities and conditions. The relevant participants and system boundary must be specified for each inquiry rather than assumed.

The existence of an interaction does not establish that the participants are equal in influence, that the relationship is reciprocal in the same degree, or that the consequences are beneficial. Those are separate questions. IFD distinguishes the relational encounter from the character and consequences of that encounter.

02 / Relational encounter

Identities, Actions, entities, and conditions

An interaction can be examined in terms of the identities or entities involved, the Actions through which they engage, the conditions under which the encounter occurs, and the changes or responses associated with it. The formulation therefore does not reduce interaction to a vague connection or to the mere co-presence of two things.

Relational field of inquiry

PARTICIPANTS

Identify the distinct identities or entities involved in the interaction.

ACTIONS

Identify what each participant does, expresses, initiates, or responds to.

CONDITIONS

Describe the relevant context, constraints, resources, and circumstances.

ENGAGEMENT

Examine the relational encounter, responses, exchanges, and changes over time.

These are useful observational distinctions, not a claim that every interaction can be reduced to four independent variables. Their purpose is to make the relationship investigable and to prevent an inference about interaction from being based solely on the attributes of the participants.

Conditions matter because the same identities may interact differently under different circumstances. Prior history, available resources, constraints, timing, and the configuration of other participants may be relevant. Their influence must be examined in the particular case; none should be treated as a universal determinant without evidence.

03 / Reciprocal engagement

Reciprocity is a question to investigate

IFD includes reciprocal engagement as a central feature to examine within interaction. Participants may respond to one another, alter their subsequent Actions, reinforce or resist a pattern, or change the conditions under which the relationship continues. Reciprocal does not necessarily mean equal, simultaneous, harmonious, or mutually beneficial.

A relationship may involve unequal initiative, asymmetric influence, delayed response, competing goals, cooperation, conflict, dependence, or withdrawal. These are possible configurations to observe, not an exhaustive typology and not predetermined outcomes. The analysis should describe what occurs rather than force the encounter into a preferred relational label.

Interaction is also temporal. A single observation may miss the development of a relationship, a shift in participant behavior, or the effect of prior encounters. Repeated observations can help distinguish a momentary exchange from a persistent pattern, provided that the observational method and time intervals are appropriate to the question.

04 / Scale and configuration

Individual, dyadic, and collective interaction

A dyad—two interacting identities—can provide a tractable configuration for investigation. It is not presumed to be the universal or optimal form of interaction. Larger configurations introduce additional relationships, coordination demands, dependencies, and possible emergent patterns. The number of participants alone does not establish the quality or outcome of the interaction.

INDIVIDUAL

An identity encounters an event, entity, environment, or set of conditions.

DYADIC

Two distinct identities engage; reciprocal and asymmetric patterns can be examined.

COLLECTIVE

Multiple identities participate in a configuration whose relational pattern may not be reducible to any one participant.

SYSTEMIC

Interactions occur across nested or overlapping systems, boundaries, and scales.

IFD investigates how coordination occurs, how individual Actions contribute to collective behavior, what patterns emerge, and which properties can be attributed to an individual versus the collective organization. A collective is not assumed to possess precisely the same properties as an individual.

05 / Collective manifestation

Distinct identities in relationship

The formulation's conceptual progression is DISTINCTION → ACTION → RELATIONSHIP → INTERACTION → COLLECTIVE PATTERN. Distinct identities participate in larger patterns of organization. The inquiry concerns how those patterns form, persist, change, and produce consequences.

The broader ambition is to understand how immense multiplicity can coexist with unity and coherence. This is a research direction, not a conclusion already demonstrated. The formulation includes cosmic expansion as a possible wider implication of increasing manifestation and differentiation; this remains a proposed cosmological consequence, not an established result of the current mathematical or empirical record.

Collective manifestation must be examined without assuming that the collective is merely the sum of individuals, or that it automatically has a unified intention or identity. Those propositions require clear definitions and evidence. Individual and collective levels should be distinguished while their relationships remain open to investigation.

06 / Relationship to the other dimensions

INTERACTION necessarily entails SYNERGY

Within the current formulation, interaction necessarily entails synergy. SYNERGY is not an optional event that may or may not follow interaction. It is the consequential relational dynamic inherent in interaction. What varies is the character, direction, degree, duration, and consequences of that synergy.

This distinction preserves the architecture of AIIST. ACTION concerns initiating and subsequent expression; IDENTITY concerns individual distinctness and manifestation; INTERACTION concerns relational encounter; SYNERGY concerns the graduated spectrum of consequences arising from that interaction; TRACE concerns what remains as consequence, residue, record, or persistence.

The operational sequence is not a set of isolated boxes. An interaction is intelligible in relation to the identities and Actions involved, and its consequences are examined through SYNERGY. Those consequences may leave TRACE, which may inform subsequent conditions and Actions. The arrows organize inquiry; they do not by themselves establish a universal one-way causal law.

For the full consequential architecture, see SYNERGY — the spectrum of interaction consequences. For the collective configuration, see Collective Manifestation.

07 / Observational and research implications

Making interaction investigable

  • Define the relational unit: identify the participants, entities, and system boundary relevant to the question.
  • Describe the encounter: record the Actions, responses, exchanges, and conditions rather than inferring interaction from personal attributes alone.
  • Preserve asymmetry: do not presume equal influence, equal contribution, or equal participation.
  • Observe across time: distinguish momentary events from persistent, changing, or recurring patterns.
  • Separate interaction from outcome: document the relational process and examine its consequences through the SYNERGY dimension.
  • Retain Trace: preserve records of the encounter and its consequences so later observations can be compared.

Operational criteria will depend on the type of interaction being studied. A dyadic human relationship, a collective organization, and an interaction between an identity and environmental conditions may require different observational units and methods. A single generic score should not be presumed to represent all these configurations.

08 / Evidence and research status

Current IFD position

Foundational formulation: INTERACTION is identity in relationship and includes engagement among identities, Actions, entities, and conditions. Dyadic and collective configurations are within scope. Interaction necessarily entails SYNERGY in the current formulation.

Proposed research directions: reciprocal engagement, relational patterns, collective manifestation, coordination, and the relationship between individual and collective behavior.

Pending operationalization and evidence: precise definitions of interaction types, system boundaries, observational units, temporal sampling, measures of reciprocity or asymmetry, and methods for attributing patterns to individual versus collective levels.

Neither the existence of a relationship nor the presence of a collective pattern establishes a particular causal mechanism or beneficial outcome. Observations must be capable of challenging the initial interpretation. The architecture remains revisable as definitions and evidence develop.
