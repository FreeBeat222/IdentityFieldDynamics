IFD Research Machinery / Instrument 01

FLAW CHART™

Failure Logging, Analysis, and Verification — an instrument for examining features and conditions, assessing their significance, and recording what should be done with them.

Working specification v1.030 October 2026Operational validation pending
Purpose

Identify the condition. Determine its significance. Choose its disposition.

FLAW CHART™ is an IFD research instrument for identifying, registering, classifying, investigating, tracking, verifying, and documenting features, conditions, inconsistencies, limitations, and defects in a theoretical structure, model, formulation, research process, or implementation when they warrant examination.

A flaw may be a defect requiring correction, a weakness with strategic utility, a limitation useful in another context, an inconsistency worth preserving for study, or a design condition calling for redesign, reinforcement, removal, or development. Its presence does not predetermine its value or disposition.

FLAW CHART™ identifies the condition. It does not dictate the disposition. Identification precedes judgment. Investigation precedes disposition. Exploitation is not endorsement. The instrument records the reasoning, evidence, and consequences; it does not presume every criticism is correct or every weakness useful.

Operating distinction: An identified condition is not automatically a defect. A proposed correction is not a verified correction. A possible advantage is not demonstrated utility. Disposition depends on evidence, context, intended use, consequences, and constraints.

01 / Operating lifecycle

Seven stages. One traceable record.

The lifecycle is iterative, not a one-way conveyor belt. New evidence can reopen investigation; verification can send a correction back for construction; an apparently resolved item can be reopened when a regression or new counterexample appears.

01

IDENTIFY

Locate a feature, condition, inconsistency, limitation, defect, opportunity, or question warranting examination.

02

REGISTER

Assign a stable ID; preserve exact location, source, version, and context.

03

CLASSIFY

Describe type, scope, dependencies, and potential significance without overstating certainty.

04

INVESTIGATE

Examine evidence, assumptions, derivations, counterexamples, and alternatives.

05

TRACK

Maintain dated history of tests, findings, decisions, revisions, and open questions.

06

VERIFY

Test the relevant claim, assessment, or proposed action; where correction is chosen, verify that it addresses the issue without creating or relocating another.

07

DISPOSITION

Record the selected treatment: correct, remove, redesign, reinforce, develop, preserve, strategically exploit, monitor, or defer.

Value & Engineering Assessment

Where does this feature work best?

Use this compact decision procedure when a feature, capability, limitation, or design choice has more than one possible use or disposition. This assessment belongs within INVESTIGATE and informs DISPOSITION; it is not an eighth lifecycle stage.

01

IDENTIFY THE FEATURE

State exactly what is being examined. Preserve its source, version, and observed behavior.

02

DEFINE THE JOB

State its intended function, beneficiary or system, and conditions of use.

03

ASSESS ITS VALUE

Examine function, research contribution, resource efficiency, alternative-use potential, and risks or unintended consequences.

04

COMPARE DISPOSITIONS

Consider correction, removal, redesign, reinforcement, development, preservation, strategic exploitation, monitoring, or deferral.

Evidence labels · apply to each assessment dimension

DEMONSTRATEDSUPPORTED BUT INCOMPLETEPLAUSIBLE / UNTESTEDUNKNOWN

These labels describe the state of evidence. They are not numerical scores and must not be aggregated into a ranking. If evidence is insufficient, preserve uncertainty and defer judgment where appropriate.

05 / VERIFY THE DECISION: Define the observable result that would support the selected disposition. Record the method, actual result, and limitations. A recommendation is not a demonstrated outcome.

02 / Classification

Classify the condition without deciding its value.

Each entry receives one primary class and may carry additional tags. Classification is descriptive; it does not decide whether the condition is harmful, useful, neutral, or worth retaining.

DEFINITION

Ambiguous, circular, inconsistent, or missing definition.

LOGICAL

Contradiction, invalid inference, hidden premise, or non sequitur.

MATHEMATICAL

Derivation, notation, calculation, or formal-property concern.

EMPIRICAL

Unsupported observation, measurement problem, failed prediction, or counterexample.

METHODOLOGICAL

Sampling, controls, operationalization, bias, or reproducibility concern.

STRUCTURAL

Missing dependency, broken relationship, scope conflict, or architecture gap.

PROVENANCE

Missing, incorrect, or unverifiable source, version, or history.

IMPLEMENTATION

Code, page, instrument, link, or deployment defect.

PRESENTATION

Wording or interface issue that affects interpretation or use.

OTHER

Specify the concern; do not force-fit it into another category.

03 / Status vocabulary

Workflow state is not truth value.

Use one controlled status at a time. Status records where the investigation stands, not whether the underlying claim is ultimately true.

OPENINVESTIGATINGCORRECTION PROPOSEDVERIFYINGPARTIALLY RESOLVEDRESOLVEDDEFERREDREJECTEDREOPENED
  • OPEN: registered; investigation not complete.
  • INVESTIGATING: active examination.
  • CORRECTION PROPOSED: candidate change exists; not yet verified.
  • VERIFYING: candidate correction is being tested.
  • PARTIALLY RESOLVED: some aspects addressed; residual issue remains.
  • RESOLVED: evidence supports that the defined issue has been addressed within stated limits.
  • DEFERRED: paused with reason and review condition.
  • REJECTED: challenge not accepted, with reasons and evidence preserved.
  • REOPENED: a previously dispositioned item is active again due to new information.

05 / Verification standard

What earns the word RESOLVED?

A correction may be marked RESOLVED only when the record contains the original issue and acceptance criteria; the exact correction and version; an appropriate verification method; observed results and supporting evidence; checks for regressions, displaced problems, or new contradictions; and remaining limitations.

If a required element is missing, retain a non-final status or explain the justified exception. “Resolved” is bounded by the tested scope. It does not certify the entire theory or system.

NO SUSPECTED FLAW IS PRESUMED PROVEN. NO PROPOSED CORRECTION IS PRESUMED SUCCESSFUL. NO FINDING ESCAPES SCRUTINY—INCLUDING THE FLAW CHART™ ITSELF.

06 / Four-channel integration

FLAW CHART™ runs across the IFD workflow.

01 · RESEARCH

Discover challenges, collect sources and observations, preserve provenance.

02 · CONSTRUCTION

Clarify definitions, map dependencies, formulate candidate corrections.

03 · VERIFICATION

Test the issue and the correction; record results and limitations.

04 · ARCHIVING

Preserve the entry, revisions, evidence, decisions, and links.

Coordination and dependency tracking support the four channels; they are not a fifth channel.

08 / Governance and limits

A working instrument, not a claim of validation.

This specification defines the intended operation of FLAW CHART™. It does not establish that the instrument is effective, complete, unbiased, or empirically validated. Its categories, workflow, and record format may be revised through documented review. Earlier records must retain provenance and change history.

A disposition is contextual and revisable. Strategic exploitation must be recorded with its purpose, anticipated benefit, foreseeable risks, constraints, and observed consequences. It must not disguise a defect, suppress contrary evidence, or imply endorsement. When evidence is insufficient, the item may remain open, be monitored, or be deferred.

The public page explains the protocol; it does not imply a live database, automatic submission, or persistent browser storage.

Operating maxim: Identification precedes judgment. Investigation precedes disposition. FLAW CHART™ identifies the condition; it does not dictate the disposition.

Harvest first. Abstract second. Canonize last.
