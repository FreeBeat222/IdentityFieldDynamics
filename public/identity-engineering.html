From Phenomenon
to Engineering.

The caveman, the Watchtower, and a candidate functional model of Identity across biological and electrical systems.

Working research · Not yet canonical

Start with what is happening.

IDENTITY IS WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT DOES.

Identity Field Dynamics begins with the phenomenon, not with a preferred explanation of it. An organism can detect a change, respond to a threat, and alter its behavior without possessing a theory of identity. The explanation comes after the operation.

The caveman sees danger and acts. We can investigate that sequence without first resolving consciousness, metaphysics, or the ultimate source of existence.

A compact functional cycle

Observe→Compute→Signal→Act→Feedback

This is a candidate engineering abstraction, not a claim that every identity always follows these steps in this exact order. It names operations that can be investigated: acquiring information, processing it, transmitting a signal, acting, and registering the consequences.

OBSERVE

Detect conditions relevant to the system and its continued operation.

COMPUTE

Process information, compare conditions, or select a response. The mechanism may differ by substrate.

SIGNAL

Make information or a state detectable to another part of the system or to another identity.

ACT + FEEDBACK

Change behavior or conditions, then register consequences that can shape subsequent action.

The Watchtower is a control point.

In the battlefield example, the Watchtower does more than look. It detects enemy movement, interprets its significance, signals the relevant elements, and communicates directions. Those elements act; the Watchtower observes the results and can signal again.

OBSERVATION → INTERPRETATION → SIGNAL → DIRECTION → ACTION → FEEDBACK

The Watchtower is therefore a functional role in an organized system—not necessarily a literal component. It links information to coordinated action. The identity also signals: through speech, behavior, status messages, or coordinated movement, its state and activity can become detectable to others.

What changes across substrates?

BIOLOGICAL

Senses, neural processing, bodily signaling, muscles, and regulatory feedback can implement parts of the cycle.

ELECTRICAL

Sensors, computation, internal messages, outputs, actuators, and system feedback can implement comparable functional roles.

The engineering question is whether shared functional principles can be defined and tested across different implementations. We do not have to establish subjective awareness to study observable processing, signaling, action, and feedback. Functional similarity does not, by itself, prove identical experience or consciousness.

Boundary, source, and collective organization

The earlier structural images remain useful as questions to investigate:

  • WELL — SOURCE: What supplies or sustains the system?
  • ARMY — ORGANIZATION: How do distinct elements function as a collective?
  • WALL — BOUNDARY: How is the system distinguished from its surroundings, and how does it regulate exchange?
  • WATCHTOWER — OBSERVATION AND SIGNALING: How is relevant information detected, communicated, and connected to action?

An army may be coordinated by command, shared purpose, allegiance, fear, or mutual care. Those mechanisms should not be collapsed into one another. The broader IFD question is how organization, signaling, and feedback produce different forms of collective coherence.

The Source and the September 1971 experience

In September 1971, during an Orange Sunshine experience, the ordinary ego dissolved and Rick recognized what he describes as THE GOD WITHIN—the Source operating from within, rather than standing outside, existence. That personal experience is part of the history of this inquiry and connects to the working idea of the Primordial Witness.

In the emerging interpretation, the Primordial Witness is proposed as Source; Love is proposed as the binding principle; and coherence is understood as an expression of that binding. Jung's collective unconscious has also been considered as a possible source-image or shared psychic foundation.

Epistemic distinction

The experience is a reported personal event and a meaningful origin point for the inquiry. The claims that a universal Primordial Witness exists, that Love binds all creation, or that Jung's collective unconscious feeds every identity remain interpretive or metaphysical propositions—not established engineering findings. They belong in the record without being mistaken for measurements or proof.

From proposition to engineering principles

To turn this model into engineering, each proposed principle needs an operational definition, observable evidence, a stated scope, and a test that could show where it fails. The same labels must not be imposed on every system simply because the analogy is appealing.

STATE

Describe the system as it currently exists and operates.

SENSING

Identify how relevant conditions are detected.

VIABILITY

Specify what conditions the system must maintain to continue operating.

FEEDBACK

Determine how consequences affect subsequent behavior or adjustment.

The next artifact should be an Engineering Principles Registry: stable IDs, operational definitions, observable indicators, applicable forms of identity, alternative explanations, and disconfirmation tests.

HARVEST FIRST. ABSTRACT SECOND. CANONIZE LAST.

That rule governs the work. We preserve the originating experience, the observations, the metaphors, the inferences, the failed ideas, and the unresolved questions as distinct traces. Then we test what can be made operational.
