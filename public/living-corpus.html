The Living Corpus.

The investigation leaves a trace. Its history is part of the evidence.

HARVEST FIRST. ABSTRACT SECOND. CANONIZE LAST.

The IFD corpus gathers observations, conversations, research memoranda, experiments, definitions, hypotheses, revisions, and unresolved questions. Its purpose is not to make the history look tidy after the fact. It is to preserve how the investigation actually developed.

Keep the layers distinct

OBSERVATION

What was noticed, recorded, or directly reported at a particular point in time.

INTERPRETATION

What a person believes the observation may mean. Interpretation remains distinguishable from the observation itself.

HYPOTHESIS

A proposition that can be examined, compared with alternatives, and where possible tested or disconfirmed.

EVIDENCE & REVISION

What supports, weakens, changes, or rejects a proposition—and the trace of how that judgement changed.

The Codex and the record

The Codex is intended to hold the discipline's organized definitions, notation, claims, constructs, and status distinctions. The Research Record preserves significant questions and methodological turns. Experiments preserve what was done and what happened. These records should link back to their sources wherever possible.

A later interpretation must not silently overwrite an earlier observation. Historical claims may be superseded or rejected, but the path by which they entered the investigation should remain visible. The archive is a means of provenance and accountability—not a substitute for analysis.

How the corpus develops

Material is harvested before abstraction. Similar records can then be compared and deduplicated without erasing meaningful differences. Only after source-traceable review should a pattern be elevated into a formal concept or canonical statement. Missing sources, uncertain dates, conflicting versions, and incomplete coverage must remain explicitly marked.

Observation → discussion → question → research → evidence → revision.
