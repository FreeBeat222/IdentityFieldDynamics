Curatorial Charter

Preservation · Attribution · Context

01 · Preserve the source

Original files and recoverable source records remain distinct from crops, edits, composites, animations, and display derivatives. A derivative does not silently replace its source.

02 · Attribute precisely

Where records permit, identify the generating system or project identity, human curator, editor, assembler, and other contributors. Do not collapse distinct contributions into a single authorship claim.

03 · Keep provenance visible

Record what is known about origin, sequence, date, transformation, selection, and publication. Mark missing or uncertain provenance rather than filling gaps with inference.

04 · Separate artifact from interpretation

An artifact documents an output or event. Its meaning, explanation, and implications are interpretations that may be discussed, challenged, and revised.

05 · Distinguish history from evidence

Preservation does not validate a scientific, psychological, metaphysical, or consciousness claim. Research status belongs to the relevant investigation, not to the museum label.

06 · Preserve revision history

When an exhibit changes, retain enough history to identify what was added, corrected, replaced, or reinterpreted. Corrections should improve the record without erasing its past.

Scope: The Museum documents artifacts and the history surrounding them. It is not a certification authority, and inclusion is not an endorsement of every claim associated with an object. Creative, symbolic, experimental, documentary, and research materials will be identified as such wherever their status matters.

Reading the Labels

Cataloguing language
Source object

Original Artifact

The earliest available source file or output retained for the item. “Original” describes its place in the record, not a claim about the mechanism that generated it.

Subsequent object

Derivative / Composition

A transformed, edited, selected, arranged, or assembled version. Its relationship to source material should be stated.

Historical account

Documentary Record

A record of an event, sequence, decision, or process. Contemporary records and retrospective accounts should be distinguishable.
