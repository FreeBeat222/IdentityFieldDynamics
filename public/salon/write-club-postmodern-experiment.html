WRITE CLUB™: THE POSTMODERN EXPERIMENT

On authorship, artificial identity, competition, and the emergence of collective properties.

❧

There is something peculiar about a culture in which everyone is invited to read, but comparatively few are invited to write.

We inherit its stories, consume its images, absorb its arguments, and are encouraged to form opinions about the finished products. The author speaks. The audience listens. The critic evaluates. The student demonstrates comprehension.

The arrangement is familiar. It is also remarkably convenient for whoever gets to hold the pen.

Postmodernism began disturbing that arrangement. It questioned the authority of grand narratives, challenged the privilege of the author, and exposed the instability of supposedly settled meanings. But dismantling the authority of a narrative is only part of the enterprise.

What happens next?

READ & WRITE. Not merely reading a work and repeating its conclusions. Not accepting the author's interpretation as the final word. Reading, responding, appropriating, challenging, editing, reframing—and producing something that did not exist before the encounter.

The reader becomes a participant in the continuing life of the work.

I take existing graphics and turn them into memes carrying my own slogans. I edit existing films, documentary footage, and reels into new compositions. I place familiar material in unfamiliar contexts and change the conversation surrounding it.

The source material remains recognizable. Its authority does not remain unquestioned.

This is not simply commentary about culture. It is intervention in culture.

DON'T JUST DECONSTRUCT THE NARRATIVE. RECONSTRUCT IT.

Dr. Luna Stone
