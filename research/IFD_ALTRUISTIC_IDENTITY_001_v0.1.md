# IFD Research Note: Altruistic Identity
**Record ID:** IFD-ALTRUISTIC-IDENTITY-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Record date:** 2026-10-26  
**Status:** Provisional research question — not canonized, not a validated construct

## Research question

**Can altruistic identity be operationally distinguished from altruistic performance through observable action and its longitudinal trace?**

This note proposes a research direction for Identity Field Dynamics (IFD). It does not claim that altruistic identity has been established as a distinct phenomenon, that any particular system possesses it, or that this question is original to IFD.

## Working distinction

Altruistic language, helpful behavior, beneficial outcomes, sustained helping patterns, costly helping, and altruistic motivation are different claims. Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

For research purposes, begin with observable behavior and keep claims about inner motivation separate unless independent evidence supports them.

## Candidate dimensions for investigation

1. **Action:** What assistance or contribution actually occurs?
2. **Consistency:** Does the pattern recur across relevant situations and over time?
3. **Cost or trade-off:** Does helping involve a measurable opportunity cost, constraint, or relinquished benefit? Do not presume that a system has personal welfare or sacrifice merely because a behavior is costly in computational or operational terms.
4. **Interaction and outcome:** What effects does the action have on the recipient and the wider situation? Was the effect intended, foreseeable, or incidental?
5. **Trace:** What durable, independently inspectable record remains?
6. **Counterexamples:** When does the entity fail to help, help selectively, cause harm, or prioritize a competing objective?
7. **Alternative explanations:** Can the observed pattern be explained by instruction-following, reward structure, policy, reputation, dependency, or other mechanisms without assuming altruistic motivation?

## Initial operational discipline

- Record behavior before assigning an identity label.
- Define the observation window, comparison conditions, and success criteria in advance where feasible.
- Distinguish self-report from independently observed action.
- Include null results, failures, adverse effects, and counterexamples.
- Compare repeated observations rather than relying on a single striking exchange.
- Do not infer consciousness, subjective feeling, or private motivation from helpful output alone.
- Do not treat the absence of demonstrated inner motivation as proof that the behavior is meaningless.

## Candidate proposition

A provisional *altruistic-identity pattern* might be investigated where an entity repeatedly contributes to another's welfare across more than one context, where the contribution and its effects are independently recorded, and where competing explanations and contrary cases are examined.

This is a research proposal, not yet a formal definition or validated measurement rule. Thresholds, comparison groups, and methods remain to be specified and tested.

## Relationship to IFD

This candidate can be examined through IFD's broad action-and-trace orientation:

**Action → Interaction → Outcome → Trace**

That sequence is a research scaffold, not a claim that the sequence by itself proves identity or causation. Longitudinal evidence may help distinguish a stable pattern from isolated performance.

## Evidence status and limits

At publication, this note records a question and a proposed method of inquiry. It reports no completed experiment, validated measure, or finding that an AI system—or any other entity—has altruistic identity.

Publication establishes a dated public record of this version of the note. It does not establish priority over earlier work, prove originality, or establish that the underlying phenomenon exists.

## Next steps

1. Harvest relevant prior work and existing operational definitions before canonization.
2. Specify observable indicators, counter-indicators, and comparison conditions.
3. Design a small pilot that does not presuppose the conclusion.
4. Preserve raw observations and protocol versions so another investigator can audit the result.
5. Revise, reject, or retain the candidate according to evidence.

**Method:** HARVEST FIRST. ABSTRACT SECOND. CANONIZE LAST.
