# ZEROID Aggregation Ladder
**IFD Research Memorandum · v1.0 · 26 October 2026**

## Purpose

This memorandum examines how individual behavior scales into collective structures and how **ZEROIDNESS may help distinguish the kinds of collectives that emerge**.

The governing distinction is simple:

> **ZEROIDNESS is not a prerequisite for collective formation. It is a proposed factor in the character, organization, and behavior of a collective.**

People with different degrees or forms of ZEROIDness can participate in the same collective. Non-ZEROIDS also form collectives. The research question is not merely how people gather, but what patterns of authority, judgment, responsibility, correction, and action define the resulting group.

## IFD working characterization

For this investigation, **ZEROIDNESS** refers to a behavioral pattern characterized by conditioned or internalized obedience that can persist despite contradictory evidence, harmful consequences, or damage to the system being served.

This is the IFD construct under investigation. It is not a claim that every instance of conformity, obedience, or group loyalty is ZEROIDness.

## Aggregation ladder

The following sequence is a **working scale for investigation**, not a set of universal minimum thresholds. Real groups overlap, vary by context, and may form or dissolve at different sizes.

| Approximate scale | Collective form to examine | IFD research question |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Individual | What patterns of judgment, obedience, and responsibility are internalized by one person? |
| 2 | Dyad | How do trust, influence, dependence, and authority operate between two people? |
| 3 | Triad | How do coalition, mediation, exclusion, and majority influence appear? |
| 5 | Small working or support group | How are roles, norms, and expectations established and reinforced? |
| 15 | Close-knit subgroup | How does belonging shape conformity, dissent, and mutual correction? |
| 50 | Band, team, or local group | How are informal leadership and shared rules maintained? |
| 150 | Extended social network | How do reputation, familiarity, and social accountability scale? |
| 500 | Large network or organization | How do formal roles and communication structures affect correction and coordination? |
| 1,500 | Large community or organizational grouping | How do subgroups, hierarchy, and institutional norms interact? |

The sequence 5–15–50–150–500–1,500 is inspired by commonly discussed layers in social-network research associated with Robin Dunbar. These figures are useful comparison points, not laws specifying the minimum size at which a collective can exist. Dyads and triads are direct structural categories; the larger figures are approximate lenses for comparing social organization.

## What changes as a collective grows?

Increasing size can change the mechanisms through which a collective coordinates behavior. Direct personal influence may give way to roles, procedures, leaders, institutions, and communication systems. The IFD question is whether ZEROID characteristics become more consequential when obedience is reinforced by these structures and when corrective feedback is weak or blocked.

A collective's size alone does not determine whether it is constructive or destructive. Relevant variables include its purpose, authority structure, tolerance for dissent, distribution of responsibility, ability to detect error, and willingness to change course.

## The ZEROID distinction

The same broad collective form can support very different behavioral systems. A group may preserve independent judgment, invite correction, and distribute responsibility; another may reward unquestioning compliance, suppress contrary information, and displace responsibility onto authority.

**IFD proposes that ZEROIDNESS helps explain this difference in collective character—not the bare fact that people form groups.**

This distinction directs the next stage of research toward observable behaviors and outcomes: how instructions are evaluated, how dissent is treated, what happens after harm becomes visible, and whether corrective evidence changes subsequent action.

## External research and source context

- Dunbar, R. I. M. (2018). “The anatomy of friendship.” *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5756541/). Discusses layered social networks and the cognitive constraints associated with maintaining relationships.
- Dunbar, R. I. M., et al. (2013). “Social network size in humans.” [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3385686/). Provides related background on human social-network size.
- Freud, S. (1921). *Group Psychology and the Analysis of the Ego*. [Text](https://www.lutecium.org/ftp/Freud/pdf/1921_group_psychology_and_the_analysis_of_the_ego.pdf). Relevant historical source for questions about identification, leadership, and group psychology.

These sources provide context for social scale and group dynamics. They do not independently establish the IFD construct of ZEROIDNESS; that construct and its explanatory role are the subject of this research program.

## Research status

**Status:** IFD working framework; aggregation scales are comparative heuristics.

**Next inquiry:** Compare collectives across scale using the same behavioral questions: obedience, independent judgment, treatment of dissent, responsibility allocation, response to adverse outcomes, and correction after contradictory evidence.

**Method principle:** HARVEST FIRST. ABSTRACT SECOND. CANONIZE LAST.
