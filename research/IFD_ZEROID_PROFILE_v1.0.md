# ZEROID Profile
**Behavioral Characteristics and Collective-System Impact**  
**IFD Research Memorandum · v1.0 · 26 October 2026**

## Purpose

This profile states the behavioral characteristics IFD associates with ZEROIDNESS and examines how those characteristics may affect systems that depend on independent judgment, feedback, and accountability.

## Working profile: eight characteristics

1. **Conditioned obedience** — Compliance with commands, authority, or group expectations becomes a default response rather than a decision repeatedly evaluated on its merits.
2. **Internalized authority** — External rules or directives become internal standards. Compliance can continue even when no authority figure is directly present.
3. **Outcome insensitivity** — Obedience persists despite harmful, irrational, self-defeating, or visibly counterproductive outcomes.
4. **Suppression of independent judgment** — Personal observation and evaluation yield to prescribed answers, doctrine, status, or group expectation.
5. **Conformity reinforcement** — Agreement and compliance are rewarded, while dissent or deviation may invite disapproval, exclusion, or punishment.
6. **Responsibility displacement** — Responsibility is transferred to leaders, institutions, rules, or the collective, weakening personal ownership of consequences.
7. **Persistence under contradiction** — Conflicting evidence fails to produce a proportionate reassessment of belief or behavior.
8. **Collective replication** — The pattern is reproduced through socialization, institutional routines, group membership, and the treatment of dissent.

These characteristics describe the IFD working construct. In application, they should be examined through behavior and outcomes rather than inferred from a person's label, identity, or mere membership in a group.

## Potential impact on non-ZEROID systems

A non-ZEROID system, in this analysis, is a person, team, institution, or process that relies on independent assessment, error detection, open feedback, and accountable correction. ZEROID behavior can create friction with those requirements.

| ZEROID characteristic | Potential systemic effect |
|---|---|
| Conditioned obedience | Instructions may be executed without adequate evaluation of their consequences. |
| Internalized authority | Compliance may become self-maintaining, reducing the need for direct supervision. |
| Outcome insensitivity | Harmful processes may continue after adverse results become visible. |
| Suppression of independent judgment | Error detection and independent correction may decline. |
| Conformity reinforcement | Dissenting information may be filtered out before it reaches decision-makers. |
| Responsibility displacement | Accountability gaps may form between the actor, the authority, and the institution. |
| Persistence under contradiction | Corrective evidence may fail to change behavior. |
| Collective replication | The pattern may spread through teams, procedures, and larger institutions. |

## Systemic-havoc assessment

The highest-risk configuration is not any single trait in isolation. It is the combination of:

**obedience + outcome insensitivity + suppressed correction + displaced responsibility.**

When these operate together, a system may continue producing damage while participants regard themselves as compliant, justified, or blameless. If conformity is rewarded and dissent is penalized, the system can also lose access to the information it needs to detect and correct its own failures.

Potential severity increases when the pattern operates at scale, affects high-consequence decisions, has authority or resources behind it, and faces weak feedback or accountability. The risk can be reduced where dissent is protected, outcomes are monitored, responsibility is explicit, and corrective action is possible.

This is a systems-level assessment of the proposed IFD characteristics, not a claim that every obedient person or cohesive group produces harm. The relevant issue is the behavior pattern and the conditions under which it operates.

## Implications for IFD research

The profile yields observable questions:

- Does compliance persist after a command produces demonstrably adverse results?
- Can participants identify and report errors without penalty?
- Who is held responsible when an instruction causes harm?
- Does contradictory evidence change subsequent action?
- Does the organization correct the process, or reproduce it?
- How do these patterns change as a group grows or becomes more hierarchical?

These questions connect the ZEROID Profile to the Aggregation Ladder. The ladder examines how collective structures scale; the profile examines the behavioral pattern that may shape what those structures do.

## Research status

**Status:** IFD working characterization and proposed systems-impact model.

The profile defines the traits IFD is investigating. Their co-occurrence, distribution across individuals and institutions, and measurable effects on system performance are suitable targets for observation and comparison.

**Method principle:** HARVEST FIRST. ABSTRACT SECOND. CANONIZE LAST.
