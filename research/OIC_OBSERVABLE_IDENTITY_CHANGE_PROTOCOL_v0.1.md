# OIC — Observable Identity Change™
## IFD Research Protocol v0.1

**Status:** Authorized working construct; initial research protocol  
**Discipline:** Identity Field Dynamics (IFD)  
**Classification:** Proposed research instrument — not empirically validated  
**Purpose:** Investigate whether observable patterns of action provide evidence consistent with change in an identity configuration.

---

## 1. Purpose

OIC investigates a central IFD question:

**What observable actions provide evidence that an identity configuration has changed over time?**

OIC records observable behavior, compares patterns across time and circumstances, and evaluates whether the evidence supports a persistent change in how a system operates.

OIC does not assume that every behavioral change represents an identity change.

## 2. Foundational principle

**An action is an observation. A repeated pattern is behavioral evidence. A change in identity configuration is an interpretation requiring further evidence.**

These levels must remain distinct:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observation | An identifiable action occurred. |
| Behavioral pattern | Repeated actions establish a recognizable pattern. |
| Configuration inference | Evidence supports a change in the system's operating configuration. |

No level automatically establishes the next.

## 3. Working definitions

- **Observable action:** An identifiable behavior or operation that can be recorded by an observer or instrument.
- **Behavioral pattern:** A recurring relationship among actions, circumstances, and outcomes.
- **Baseline:** The documented pattern of behavior before an intervention or comparison period.
- **Configuration:** The provisional organization of behaviors and relationships being investigated.
- **Configuration change:** A proposed alteration in the organization or persistence of a behavioral pattern, supported by observations across time or circumstances.
- **Trace:** A record or consequence left by an action or interaction that can be examined later.
- **Context:** The environmental, social, procedural, or situational conditions in which an action occurs.

## 4. Six initial observation domains

OIC begins with six proposed domains. They are observation categories, not independent laws or a validated psychological scale.

### OIC-01 — Consistency
Does a behavior recur under comparable circumstances?

Examples: keeping commitments, completing agreed tasks, following established procedures.

### OIC-02 — Decision-making
Does the pattern of choices change?

Examples: moving from avoidance to acceptance of responsibility; selecting different courses of action when alternatives are available.

### OIC-03 — Response to pressure
How does behavior change under difficulty, uncertainty, or competing demands?

Examples: continuing a task after an obstacle; seeking assistance rather than abandoning the task; changing strategy when the original approach fails.

### OIC-04 — Interaction
Does behavior toward other people or systems change?

Examples: initiating cooperation; responding differently to requests; sharing information or resources.

### OIC-05 — Recovery and correction
What happens after an error, failure, or interruption?

Examples: acknowledging an error; correcting it; modifying subsequent behavior to reduce recurrence.

### OIC-06 — Persistence across circumstances
Does the observed pattern continue over time and in different situations?

Examples: behavior recurring rather than appearing once; a pattern continuing when immediate rewards or supervision are absent; a response transferring to a different but relevant task.

## 5. Observation protocol

Each OIC investigation follows six stages.

### Stage 1 — Establish the baseline
Document behavior before the proposed change. Record the target action, circumstances, frequency, opportunities, and constraints.

### Stage 2 — Define the observation
Specify the behavior before collecting results. Avoid vague descriptions such as “became more responsible.” Prefer observable descriptions such as “completed four of five agreed tasks by the specified deadline.”

### Stage 3 — Record actions
Record what happened. Separate direct observation, participant self-report, records or artifacts, and observer interpretation.

### Stage 4 — Repeat observations
Observe the same behavior across multiple occasions. Where appropriate, vary circumstances to determine whether the behavior persists beyond the original situation.

### Stage 5 — Compare
Compare baseline and subsequent observations. Identify what changed, what remained consistent, when the difference appeared, whether it persisted, and what alternative explanations might account for it.

### Stage 6 — Evaluate
Determine the strongest conclusion justified by the evidence.

Possible outcomes:
1. No observable change.
2. Temporary behavioral variation.
3. Repeated behavioral change.
4. Persistent behavioral change across relevant circumstances.
5. Evidence consistent with a possible identity-configuration change.
6. Insufficient evidence to determine.

OIC must permit an inconclusive result.

## 6. Four evaluation tests

| Test | Research question |
|---|---|
| Repetition | Has the behavior occurred repeatedly? |
| Duration | Has it persisted over time? |
| Variation | Does it occur under different relevant circumstances? |
| Consequence | Does it produce a discernible difference in subsequent action, interaction, or outcome? |

These tests strengthen an interpretation but do not independently prove an identity change.

## 7. Observation record

Every OIC trial should preserve:

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Investigation ID | Unique reference |
| Observation date | Date and time |
| Subject/system | Participant or system identifier |
| Target behavior | Precisely defined action |
| Context | Conditions and circumstances |
| Baseline | Previously observed behavior |
| Current observation | What actually happened |
| Frequency | Number of occurrences/opportunities |
| Outcome | Observable consequence |
| Evidence source | Observer, record, instrument, or report |
| Alternative explanations | Relevant competing interpretations |
| Assessment | Result and confidence limitations |

Records should preserve original observations separately from subsequent interpretation.

## 8. Initial pilot experiment

**Pilot OIC-001 — Repeated Task Completion**

**Objective:** Determine whether a previously observed behavioral pattern changes and persists following a change in task circumstances.

**Procedure:**
1. Select a simple, harmless task with a clearly defined completion condition.
2. Establish a baseline across repeated opportunities.
3. Introduce a modest, documented change in circumstances.
4. Record whether the participant completes the task.
5. Repeat observations under comparable conditions.
6. Introduce a relevant variation in circumstances.
7. Compare completion patterns, timing, corrections, and outcomes.

**Example:** A participant is asked to complete an agreed task by a specified time. The investigator records whether it was completed, whether it was completed on time, whether assistance was requested, whether an obstacle was addressed, whether the behavior recurred, and whether the pattern persisted when circumstances changed.

The experiment does not classify the participant as reliable or unreliable. It investigates whether a measurable behavioral pattern changed.

## 9. Measurement and analysis rules

OIC v0.1 does not authorize a composite identity score.

Do not:
- Add the six observation domains into an arbitrary identity number.
- Assume that all domains have equal importance.
- Treat one successful action as proof of lasting change.
- Interpret every behavioral difference as identity development.
- Infer an internal state solely from an external action.

Where data permit, report counts and proportions, changes in frequency, time intervals, repeated observations, differences between conditions, and missing observations or uncertainty.

For multiple observers, examine whether they independently classify the same actions consistently. Where possible, predefine behavior and evaluation criteria before observing results.

## 10. Alternative explanations and limitations

A behavioral change may result from factors other than a change in identity configuration. Investigators should consider:
- Different opportunities.
- New skills or knowledge.
- Environmental changes.
- Incentives and rewards.
- External pressure.
- Fatigue or temporary conditions.
- Changes in available resources.
- Measurement or observer error.

OIC must not confuse correlation with causation.

A persistent behavioral change is evidence worth investigating, not automatic proof of a new identity configuration.

## 11. Ethical and operational safeguards

Research involving people must use appropriate consent, privacy protection, and safeguards against coercion. Collect only information necessary for the stated investigation.

Do not use OIC v0.1 to diagnose individuals, rank their worth, make employment or eligibility decisions, or impose permanent identity labels.

For artificial systems, record the system version, configuration, prompts or inputs, environmental conditions, and relevant execution settings. A change in output may reflect a system update or altered input rather than a change in identity.

## 12. Relationship to the IFD architecture

OIC is designed to investigate a specific part of the IFD research sequence:

**Identity → Action → Interaction → Synergy → Trace**

Its initial contribution is to examine the relationship between action, repeated behavior, and trace over time.

It helps distinguish:
- What a system says about itself.
- What a system actually does.
- What an observer records.
- What consequences follow.
- What conclusions the evidence supports.

OIC does not replace OBSERVOR or the IFD Quiz Lab. It is a distinct research protocol that may eventually complement those instruments.

## 13. Relationship to the Euler Machine

The Euler Machine provides a mathematical illustration of continuous change and distinguishable classifications.

OIC addresses a different question:

**Can changes in an identity configuration be investigated through observable actions over time?**

The two may eventually inform each other, but OIC does not assume that identity literally rotates, follows a circular trajectory, or obeys Euler's equations.

Mathematical models must be justified independently of behavioral observations.

## 14. Validation requirements

Before OIC can be described as a validated research instrument, it requires:
1. Clear operational definitions.
2. Repeatable observation procedures.
3. Evidence of agreement between independent observers.
4. Evidence that the indicators measure what they are intended to investigate.
5. Examination of alternative explanations.
6. Replication across appropriate tasks and contexts.
7. Documentation of failures, ambiguities, and limitations.

A successful pilot demonstrates that the procedure can be conducted and produces interpretable observations. It does not, by itself, validate the underlying theory.

## 15. Development roadmap

**OIC v0.1 — Initial protocol**
- Establish the construct.
- Define the observation domains.
- Establish evidence distinctions.
- Specify the initial pilot.

**OIC v0.2 — Pilot refinement**
- Conduct and document pilot observations.
- Identify ambiguous definitions.
- Improve recording procedures.
- Examine observer agreement.

**OIC v0.3 — Reliability and validity review**
- Test repeatability.
- Compare observation methods.
- Evaluate alternative explanations.
- Document limitations and failed assumptions.

**OIC v1.0 — Candidate formal instrument**
- Publish only after the procedures and validation requirements have been reviewed.
- Preserve the distinction between validated procedures and unvalidated theoretical interpretations.

---

## Governing statement

**OIC investigates observable changes in behavior as potential evidence of changes in identity configuration.**

It does not presume that behavior fully reveals identity, that every behavioral change constitutes identity change, or that a proposed identity configuration has been established merely because it can be described.

Its governing commitment is:

**Observe what happens. Record what happens. Compare what happens. Conclude only what the evidence permits.**

*OIC — Observable Identity Change™ is an authorized IFD working construct. Protocol v0.1 is a proposed research instrument pending pilot testing and validation.*
